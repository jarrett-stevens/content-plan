Content Architecture

Three days. Three jobs. One arc.

Monday earns attention by naming what your client already feels. Wednesday builds authority through teaching the Novus and BHP framework. Friday does double duty — it transfers credibility through story, then closes with a sharp provocation that drives saves, shares, and DMs. Three posts a week, nothing wasted.

Monday — The Mirror
Name what they feel
Describes your client's inner world with such accuracy they feel seen before you've said a word about coaching. Carousel or single image. No framework language — pure empathy and observation.
Earns trust
Wednesday — The Teaching
Show the framework
One Novus or BHP concept made concrete. Always a video reel — your voice and presence do the teaching. Positions you as the thinker behind the transformation, not just a coach with an opinion.
Builds authority
Friday — Proof + Provocation
Story, then reframe
First half: a real story — pastoral or coaching — that transfers 30 years of credibility into this context. Second half: a sharp provocation or question that makes the post worth saving, sharing, or responding to.
Transfers trust + drives engagement

Week 1 Theme

The Invisible Ceiling

Name the problem no one else is naming. Every post this week holds a mirror up to the high-achieving leader who has outwardly won but privately wonders if this is all there is. You're not pitching coaching — you're describing someone's inner life with such accuracy they feel compelled to follow.

Week 1 Hashtag Set

Monday Mirror
Carousel · 4 slides
"You've achieved more than most people dream of. So why does it feel like something is still missing?"
Caption

There's a version of success that looks exactly right from the outside.

The title. The income. The team that respects you. The life that checks every box you were told to check.

And then there's the quiet voice — usually somewhere around 11pm on a Tuesday — that says: Is this it?

That voice isn't ingratitude. It's not a midlife crisis. It's not you being difficult or selfish or broken.

That voice is a signal. And most high achievers have been trained their whole lives to ignore it.

What if the feeling isn't a problem to solve? What if it's an invitation?

Visual Direction
Slide 1: Bold hook text on dark slate background. Large serif font. No image.
Slides 2–3: Warm cream background. One short phrase per slide. Generous white space.
Slide 4: Your face — candid, direct eye contact. Minimal overlay: "That voice is a signal."
Palette: Slate + cream + single gold accent. No stock photos. No icons.
CTA
Save this if it landed somewhere real. Drop a 🔥 if you've felt this.
Lens Invisible ceiling concept — pure empathy, no framework language
Wednesday Teach
Video Reel · 60–90 sec
"High performers ask: how can I be the best? Meta performers ask something completely different."
Video Script
[Open to camera, direct eye contact, slight lean forward — no intro pleasantries]

Here's a question that changed how I think about achievement.

What's the difference between a high performer and a meta performer?

[pause — one beat]

A high performer asks: how can I be the best?
A meta performer asks: what am I actually capable of?

[slight shift — this is the turn]

Those sound similar. They're not.

"Being the best" is a comparison. It's external. It has a ceiling.
"What am I capable of" — that's a different conversation entirely.

There's an image I come back to all the time: a mother lifting a car off her child. In that moment, she wasn't thinking about being the best. She was operating from something she didn't even know she had.

[quieter, slower]

That's meta performance. And most high achievers have never actually gone there.

[direct to camera]

What would it look like if you stopped trying to be the best — and started asking what you're actually capable of?

[hold — no outro, just the question]
Visual Direction
Seated. Good window light on one side. Dark or neutral background. No ring light.
Medium shot, slightly below eye level. Lean forward on the key line.
Text overlay at open: "High performer vs. Meta performer" — small, bottom third.
No music. Silence gives the question room to land.
Lens BHP Performance Pyramid — Meta Performance™, the mother/car image

Week 2 Theme

Identity Before Strategy

Draw the line between performance coaching and transformation coaching. This week you introduce the inside-out framework — and you position your pastoral background not as an aside, but as the source of your deepest coaching insight. A leader who follows all three posts this week understands, intuitively, that you are not a tactics coach.

Week 2 Hashtag Set
#IdentityFirst #TransformationCoaching #ExecutiveCoach #LeadershipIdentity #InsideOut #LeadershipMindset #CEOMindset #NovusGlobal #BeyondHighPerformance #PurposeDrivenLeader #LeadershipCoach
Monday Mirror
Carousel · 5 slides
"Everyone told you the promotion would feel different. It didn't. Here's why."
Caption

You hit the number. Made the hire. Closed the deal. Led the team through the hard quarter.

And then you sat in the quiet afterwards — and nothing had shifted.

Not because you're ungrateful. Not because you're broken. But because external wins don't touch the internal program.

You can win the game on the outside and still be running an old version of yourself on the inside.

That's not a performance problem. It's an identity problem. And no promotion, bonus, or achievement will solve it.

The work that changes everything doesn't start with strategy. It starts with: Who do I need to become?

Visual Direction
Carousel: alternate cream and slate slides. One phrase per slide in large serif.
Final slide: your face + the question "Who do I need to become?" as the only text.
Pacing: each slide should feel like a beat in a sermon. Slow. Weight on every line.

Wednesday Teach
Video Reel · 60–90 sec
"There are four ways a leader can relate to their work. Only one of them is actually sustainable."
Video Script
[Direct to camera. Lean back slightly — relaxed, confident, not urgent]

Quick diagnostic question. And I want you to answer honestly.

How do you relate to your work right now?

There are four postures a leader can take.

The Prisoner. Their question is: what's the least I can do? They're there — but they're not there.

The Mercenary. Pure transaction. What's in it for me? They perform — but only when the incentive is right.

The Missionary. All cause, all the time. They'll burn themselves to the ground for the mission — and eventually they do.

The Athlete. The Athlete treats everything as practice. Not just the wins — the losses, the ordinary Tuesdays, all of it. All of it is data. All of it is development.

[pause — slower]

Here's the honest question: which one showed up in your Monday morning this week?

[hold — no answer, no wrap-up]
Visual Direction
Standing for this one — slightly more energy than seated. Clean background.
Text overlays: each of the four names appears as you say it. Simple lower-third style.
End on stillness. Don't move after the final question. Let the camera hold.
Lens BHP — Four Relationships to Work: Prisoner, Mercenary, Missionary, Athlete
Friday Proof + Provocation
Single Image · Long Caption
"A leader came to me wanting a better strategy. What they actually needed was a different story about themselves."
The Proof

He ran a $40M division. Hit his numbers three years running. Came in saying: I need a better system for managing up.

Twenty minutes in, we weren't talking about managing up. We were talking about a story he'd carried since he was twelve — that he had to earn his place in every room.

That story made him exceptional. It also exhausted him. And it was the ceiling.

We spent six months not building a better system. We spent it dismantling the story that made a system necessary.

By the end, he wasn't managing up differently. He was walking into rooms differently.

The Provocation

You don't have a strategy problem. You have a self-concept problem.

Strategy is the last thing a stuck leader needs. It's the first thing they ask for.

What story are you still carrying that you've never actually examined?

Visual Direction
You in coaching posture — seated across from someone (or positioned as if you are). Listening.
Warm light. Feels like a real room, not a studio. No text overlay.
CTA
What story are you still carrying that you've never examined? DMs open.
Lens Transformation coaching — identity over tactics. Composite client story.

The Tools That Actually Work

Shift from naming the problem to demonstrating the tools. Give the audience a real taste of what it's like to be coached by you. Use Novus and BHP language naturally — not as jargon, but as a vocabulary your audience starts to adopt. By Friday, they should be using phrases like "complaint as vision" without knowing where they got it.

Monday Mirror
Single Image · Medium Caption
"The most dangerous thing a high achiever does is blame themselves for the wrong thing."
Caption

Something is off. You can feel it.

So you do what high achievers do — you diagnose and fix. Work harder. Optimize the process. Read the book.

And six months later, something is still off.

Most stuck leaders aren't broken. They're misdiagnosed. They're treating the symptom and missing the root.

The frustration isn't about productivity. The restlessness isn't about work-life balance. The ceiling isn't about strategy.

It almost always goes deeper. And the tools that got you to the top were not designed to help you see below the surface. That's not a flaw. That's physics. You need a different instrument.

Wednesday Teach
Video Reel · 60–75 sec
"Every complaint you have as a leader is actually a vision you haven't claimed yet."
Video Script
[Seated. Closer to camera than usual. Conversational — like you're sharing something you just realized]

Here's a reframe that changes everything in a coaching room.

Every complaint a leader has — every one — is a vision they haven't claimed yet.

[pause — let them sit with it]

When you say "nobody on my team takes initiative" — that's not a complaint. That's a vision for a team that does.

When you say "I'm not making the kind of impact I want to make" — that's not frustration. That's a vision for the impact you're capable of.

When you say "I feel like I'm playing it safe" — that's not weakness. That's a vision for a version of you who doesn't.

[lean in slightly]

Your frustrations are not problems. They're a map.
Most leaders just never learn to read them.

What's the complaint you keep coming back to? Because under it, there's a vision waiting.

[hold — no resolution, just the question]
Visual Direction
Closer framing than Weeks 1–2 videos. Trust is building — the camera can come in.
Text overlays: each "When you say..." line gets a subtle lower-third reinforcement.
Warm light, slightly softer. Less confrontational, more invitational.
Lens Novus Global — complaint as latent vision
Friday Proof + Provocation
Video Reel · 90 sec
"Thirty years of preaching taught me something about what actually changes people. It's not the content."
Video Script
[Standing. More space behind you — authority post. Bookshelves or a meaningful room.]

I've been delivering content to people my whole career. Sermons. Books. Coaching sessions. Conference talks.

And here's what thirty years of watching people change — or not change — has taught me:

The content is almost never the variable.

[pause]

I've watched people sit through the most accurate truth of their lives — and walk out exactly the same as they walked in.

And I've watched a single question — asked at exactly the right moment — dismantle a belief someone has carried for forty years.

The difference was never the words. It was the presence behind the words. The willingness to go first. The room that was created for something to actually happen.

[slower, quieter]

The best preachers and the best coaches share one thing: they're not performing. They're being.

[slight pause, then the provocation]

So here's what I want you to sit with this weekend:
What are you most frustrated about right now?
Because under that frustration — there's a map. And on that map, there's a vision you haven't claimed yet.

[hold — end there]
Visual Direction
Most authority visual of the series. A real room — bookshelves, meaningful space, not a backdrop.
Minimal text overlays — only the final question gets text: "What are you most frustrated about?"
This post: invite comments. Respond to each one publicly — name the latent vision you see underneath. Coaching demo in public.
CTA
Drop your frustration in the comments. I'll tell you what I see under it. High-engagement play — respond to every reply.
Lens Pastoral authority transfer + complaint as latent vision (Novus). Public coaching demo.

Week 4 Theme

The Invitation

You've named the problem, introduced the framework, shown it in action. Now you issue the invitation — not as a pitch, but as a calling forward. This is where your pastoral voice is the most powerful asset in the room. A leader who has followed all four weeks should feel, by Friday, like they've already started working with you.

Week 4 Hashtag Set
#ThrillingVision #MetaPerformance #ExecutiveCoach #WhatAmICapableOf #LeadershipTransformation #NovusGlobal #BeyondHighPerformance #TransformationalLeadership #PurposeDriven #LeadershipCoach #CalledForward
Monday Mirror
Carousel · 4 slides
"The leaders I work with aren't broken. They're just operating on a program written for an earlier version of themselves."
Caption

You built a playbook that was brilliant. It was built for the problems you had then, the resources you had then, the person you were then.

And it worked. It got you here.

But playbooks are written for a specific game. And the game has changed. You've changed. The scale has changed. The stakes have changed.

The strategies that made you exceptional are now the ceiling on what's possible.

This isn't a character flaw. It's physics. Every operating system eventually needs an upgrade.

The question isn't what got you here. It's who you need to become to go further.

Visual Direction
Warm and personal — feels like a letter. More intimate than Week 1's confrontational opener.
Final slide: a full photo of you. Not a quote card — you. Looking forward.
This post should feel like the summary of a relationship you've been building for a month.
Lens Invisible ceiling — identity evolution, operating system upgrade
Wednesday Teach
Video Reel · 75–90 sec
"A great coach doesn't help you get what you want. They help you want something bigger."
Video Script
[Seated. Most relaxed of all four weeks — you've earned it. Speaking like a trusted friend.]

I want to tell you something about what coaching actually is — because most people have it wrong.

Most people think a coach helps you get what you want. And a good tactics coach does that.

But a transformation coach? A transformation coach helps you want something bigger.

[pause — let that land]

A tactics coach asks: how do I help you win the game you're already playing?
A transformation coach asks: are you playing the right game?

Most of the leaders I work with are winning games they don't even want to win anymore. They're optimizing for a version of success that stopped feeling true somewhere along the way.

[lean forward — this is the invitation]

There's a phrase I use called a Thrilling Vision. Not a five-year plan. Not a SMART goal. A vision that actually creates goosebumps — that's worth the disruption of getting there.

Most people have never named one. Because naming it means you're responsible for it.

So let me ask you: what are you actually capable of?
Not what's realistic. Not what's safe.
What are you capable of?

[hold — quiet — end there]
Visual Direction
Warmest visual of the four weeks. You're not selling — you're inviting.
Text overlay at end: "What are you actually capable of?" — the one question that defines the whole framework.
Consider a very subtle warm music bed — just underneath, just enough to feel the shift in tone.
Lens Novus — Thrilling Vision. Transformation vs. improvement. The meta performance question.
Friday Proof + Soft CTA
Single Image · Story Caption
"I didn't know I could do that."
The Proof

There's an image I come back to constantly when I think about why I do this work.

A mother lifts a car off her child. And in the aftermath, she says: I didn't know I could do that.

That moment — the discovery of a capability she didn't know existed — that's what I'm after in every coaching room I sit in.

I've watched a leader who had run the same company for sixteen years discover a completely different version of what his leadership could be — and sit in stunned silence for thirty seconds in the middle of a session.

I've watched someone name a vision they'd been suppressing for a decade and start crying before they finished the sentence. Not from sadness. From recognition.

That's not coaching. That's someone meeting themselves — maybe for the first time. That's what I mean by transformation. Not improvement. Not optimization. A new form.

If this month of content has landed somewhere real — if any of it has felt like someone was describing your inner life — I'd love to have a conversation.

No pitch. No agenda. Just two people asking the most important question a leader can ask.

My DMs are open.
